Source-only preparation · Quality and integrity

Read a diagnostic with its coverage and source evidence

A Quality report describes the published digitization and bounded image observations. Interpret its counts, limitations and source claims together, without converting them into a claim of measurement accuracy.

Separate a useful warning from an accuracy estimate

The documented report includes curve/segment structure, calibration observations, local image evidence and issue summaries. Invalid calibration geometry, out-of-image points, repeated consecutive points, long segments, weak contrast or a monotonicity issue can help locate a problem worth inspecting.

Absence of a warning does not identify the correct scientific curve. A dark grid line, annotation, overlapping trace or wrong branch can still look locally convincing. Image-space diagnostics do not independently establish that the raster faithfully represents the original physical measurement.

The current vendor Quality documentation describes accuracy and uncertainty as not_assessed. A separately enabled uncertainty declaration belongs to its own scientific contract. Do not treat a declaration, a small corner reprojection residual or descriptive spread across calibration realizations as independent proof of accuracy.

The documented image evidence uses the canonical source raster, rather than GUI opacity or a display filter. Publishing a processed image and deliberately selecting that new Dataset as the source establishes a different canonical source context. Preserve the actual identity and lineage; see processed-image publication.

A report's coverage and validity statements are part of its scientific interpretation. Do not label an interval error-free merely because it has no retained record. A missing report or unsupported schema also remains missing/unsupported, rather than becoming a perfect score or a zero.

Keep per-segment locations and curve/calibration identifiers when following a warning back to the image. A segment reference describes original consecutive raster points; it is not automatically an index into a replicated multi-calibration table.

Unique raster points and scientific rows answer different questions

One accepted original point may be transformed by more than one calibration. A combined scientific Dataset can therefore contain more rows than there are unique original raster points. Retain the actual curve, point and calibration identities when interpreting counts.

For an illustrative curve with three unique raster points and two published calibrations, a combined output can have six scientific rows. That does not create six independent operator clicks or six independent physical measurements. The calibration-realization explanation shows this conceptual distinction.

Scientific-space plots must preserve both curve and calibration grouping. A source-raster overlay can de-duplicate the same original point by its curve/point identity. De-duplication for display must not erase distinct scientific calibration realizations or rewrite canonical published rows.

The report is a publication-time snapshot. Later edits to a current configuration do not silently update the old Dataset or its diagnostics. Select the exact intended published output; preserve its source and accepted-calibration context.

Keep the result and its limitations together

  1. Identify the exact published Dataset, source bytes and published calibration set.
  2. Check report availability/schema, total/returned counts, truncation and sampling policy.
  3. Inspect the relevant curve/segment location against the intended source, including ambiguous or weakly sampled regions.
  4. Distinguish provider-returned information from any separately qualified, time-bound CORE observation.
  5. Keep independent reference evidence and justified uncertainty separate from descriptive Quality and stroke-width diagnostics.
  6. Preserve unsupported, missing, stale or refused cases explicitly with their actual reason.
